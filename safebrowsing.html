Safe Internet Browsing

Browse Smarter. Protect Your Privacy. Stay Secure.

Every time you visit a website, share a photo, connect to Wi-Fi, or interact online, you leave behind information that can affect your digital security. While the internet provides endless opportunities for communication, education, and entertainment, unsafe browsing habits can expose personal information and create opportunities for cybercriminals.

Safe browsing is about more than avoiding suspicious websites. It means understanding your digital footprint, protecting your personal information, and making smart decisions whenever you connect to the internet.

Our goal is to help students develop safer browsing habits and take control of their digital privacy.

Protect Your Digital Footprint

Your digital footprint is the trail of information you leave behind while using the internet. Social media posts, shared photos, location check-ins, and online profiles can reveal more about you than you might realize.

Cybercriminals and third-party data collectors can use this information to build detailed profiles, track online activity, and create targeted scams. Even seemingly harmless information can become a security risk when combined with other publicly available details.

Here are a few ways to protect your digital footprint:

  • Review Privacy Settings: Regularly check your social media and application privacy settings to control who can access your information.
  • Limit Location Sharing: Avoid publicly sharing your real-time location, daily routines, or frequently visited places.
  • Think Before You Post: Be careful when sharing photos, documents, or personal details that could reveal sensitive information.
  • Manage App Permissions: Review which applications have access to your camera, microphone, contacts, and location.
  • Protect Your Online Reputation: Remember that information shared online can remain accessible long after it is originally posted.

Stay Safe on Public Wi-Fi

Connecting to free Wi-Fi at a coffee shop, library, airport, or college campus is convenient, but public networks can introduce additional security risks.

Unsecured or fraudulent wireless networks can allow attackers to monitor network activity, redirect users to malicious websites, or attempt to intercept sensitive information. Some cybercriminals even create fake Wi-Fi hotspots that imitate legitimate networks to trick users into connecting.

Follow these precautions when using public Wi-Fi:

  • Verify the Network: Confirm the official network name with the establishment before connecting.
  • Look for Secure Connections: Make sure websites use HTTPS before entering passwords or personal information.
  • Use a Trusted VPN: A reputable Virtual Private Network (VPN) can help protect your internet traffic when using untrusted networks.
  • Keep Devices Updated: Install operating system, browser, and security software updates to protect against known vulnerabilities.
  • Disable Automatic Connections: Prevent your devices from automatically connecting to unfamiliar wireless networks.
  • Enable Your Firewall: Use your device's built-in firewall to help block unauthorized network access.

Understand Modern Online Risks

Online threats continue to evolve, making it important to understand that basic privacy settings and familiar security indicators do not guarantee complete protection.

Data brokers can collect information from multiple sources, including websites, applications, and publicly available profiles. Fraudulent Wi-Fi hotspots can imitate trusted networks, while outdated software can leave devices vulnerable to security attacks.

Even photos and documents can unintentionally expose sensitive information through embedded metadata, visible account details, or location information.

Being aware of these risks helps you make better decisions about what you share, where you connect, and how you protect your devices.

Make Safe Browsing a Daily Habit

Practicing safe browsing doesn't require advanced technical knowledge. Small changes in your everyday online behavior can significantly reduce your exposure to cybersecurity threats.

Whether you're completing coursework, checking social media, shopping online, or connecting to campus Wi-Fi, staying aware of your digital surroundings is essential.

Your information is valuable. Protect your privacy, secure your devices, and think before you share.
