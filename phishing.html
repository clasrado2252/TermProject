🎣 Phishing Awareness 🎣

Learn to spot fraudulent emails, suspicious messages, and deceptive links before they compromise your personal information. Not everything you receive online is what it seems.

Think Before You Click. Don't Let Scammers Take the Bait.

Not every email, text message, or online notification is what it appears to be. Phishing is one of the most common forms of cybercrime, using deceptive messages to trick people into revealing sensitive information, clicking malicious links, or giving cybercriminals access to their accounts.

College students are frequent targets of phishing attempts disguised as legitimate university communications. Messages about financial aid, password resets, assignment grades, campus account updates, and job opportunities can all be used to create a false sense of trust and urgency.

Learning to recognize the warning signs is your first line of defense.

How to Recognize a Phishing Attempt

Phishing messages are designed to look legitimate, but many contain clues that can help you identify a potential scam.

  • Suspicious Sender Addresses:

    Check whether the sender's email address actually belongs to the organization they claim to represent.

  • Urgent or Threatening Language: Be cautious of messages demanding immediate action or threatening account suspension, lost access, or missed payments.
  • Unexpected Links: Avoid clicking unfamiliar links. Hover over them to inspect the destination before visiting a website.
  • Unusual Attachments: Unexpected files or attachments may contain malicious software designed to compromise your device.
  • Requests for Personal Information: Legitimate organizations should not ask you to disclose passwords or sensitive account information through unsolicited messages.

Phishing Is Becoming More Sophisticated

Cybercriminals are constantly developing new ways to make fraudulent communications harder to recognize. Modern phishing attacks can go far beyond poorly written emails and suspicious-looking links.

Artificial intelligence allows attackers to create convincing messages that imitate university staff, professors, and trusted organizations. Some scams even use nearly identical website addresses and legitimate-looking login pages to steal account information.

Other attacks exploit trusted authentication services or trick users into granting malicious applications access to their email, documents, and personal data.

This means that even a message that looks professional or appears to come from a familiar source cannot automatically be trusted.

Protect Yourself From Phishing

Staying safe online requires more than simply recognizing suspicious emails. Developing good digital habits can help prevent attackers from gaining access to your information.

  1. Pause Before Clicking: Never rush to open links or attachments, especially when a message creates a sense of urgency.
  2. Verify the Sender: Contact the organization directly using an official website or known contact information if a message seems suspicious.
  3. Avoid Sharing Credentials: Never provide your password or authentication codes in response to an unsolicited request.
  4. Use Multifactor Authentication: Add another layer of protection to your accounts to make unauthorized access more difficult.
  5. Report Suspicious Messages: If you receive a suspected phishing email, report it to your institution's IT or cybersecurity department rather than forwarding it to others.

Don't Take the Bait

Phishing attacks succeed when people trust messages without questioning their legitimacy. Taking a few extra seconds to inspect a message, verify its source, and think before clicking can make a significant difference in protecting your personal information.

Cybercriminals are counting on you to act without thinking. Don't give them that opportunity.

Recognize the signs. Question the message. Protect your information.
